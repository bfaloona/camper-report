# Trait picker: bullet classification and trait vocabulary

Deliverable 1 of the trait-picker plan (see `docs/trait-picker-plan.md`). Every
`camper_pros` / `camper_cons` bullet across the 25 vehicles in `vehicles.json`,
classified into the three buckets from the brief, plus the resulting pickable
trait vocabulary. Generated by script from `vehicles.json` (bullet text is
verbatim; tallies are computed from the classification rows, not hand-counted).

**Status: reviewed by Brandon on 2026-08-17. The open questions below are
resolved (see Decisions) and implementation is approved.**

| R — researched only | 25 | 10% |
| E — editorial | 59 | 24% |

"Over half derived" from the brief holds. A bullet's bucket covers only its
*mappable* ideas; leftover prose that no trait can carry ("strong resale",
"known transmission failures") is marked as *residue* in the trait column and
stays editorial supporting text regardless of the bullet's bucket.

## Trait vocabulary (the pickable list)

Each row is one picker entry: a YES / IGNORE / NO toggle. YES compiles to
`{kind:'hard', tier:'must-have', rule:{field, op:'in', value: yes-values}}`;
NO to the same rule with `tier:'deal-breaker'`; IGNORE emits nothing. All
fields are scalar enums, per the brief. `null` field values gate as `unknown`
and never exclude, in either direction.

### Group A — over existing fields (no new data, no new formulas)

| # | Picker row | Field | YES matches | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | AWD or 4WD | `drivetrain_bucket` | `['awd']` | |
| A2 | Flat sleeping floor (folds flat or seats remove) | `rear_seat_fold` | `['flat','removable']` | YES-only row: NO would mean "rule out flat-floor vehicles", a control with no sane use. `TRAITS` entries declare which sides are meaningful; A2, B2 and B7 render without a NO button |
| A3 | Folds flat with no seat removal | `rear_seat_fold` | `['flat']` | Stow 'n Go and fold-into-floor vans |
| A4–A13 | The ten equipment traits (own collapsed "Equipment" subsection in the picker, per review addition R2 — comfort features, not camper traits, and 13 rows would drown the 9 camping rows) | `heated_front_seats`, `heated_steering_wheel`, `ventilated_front_seats`, `dual_zone_climate`, `remote_start`, `sunroof`, `roof_rails`, `power_liftgate`, `cargo_power_outlet`, `fold_flat_passenger` | `['yes']` each | Already yes/no enums in `FIELDS`; the picker is just one-click access |

## Flags: where I disagree with or deviate from the brief

- **F1 — one researched field is numeric, not yes/no/enum.** The brief shapes
  researched fields as `true/false/null`. Ground clearance is the most-cited
  spec in the whole bullet corpus (~20 bullets) and is manufacturer-published.
  Storing the number (`ground_clearance_in`) and deriving the enum (B9) keeps
  the stored fact objective and moves the arguable cutoffs into an inspectable
  formula. Forcing it to an enum at research time would bake the cutoffs into
  25 records instead of one line of code.
- **F2 — kit-ecosystem depth uses `camper_popularity.rating`, not
  `conversion_products.length` bucketed** (the brief's example). The count
  measures how many products the research pass listed, not market depth: the
  Forester Hybrid has 2 entries and one is a spare-tire kit; the 4Runner's
  "biggest overland aftermarket here" has 3 rows. `camper_popularity.rating` is
  the field whose `evidence` already answers this question. `conversion_kit_count`
  stays available as a numeric criterion for anyone who wants the raw count.
- **F3 — stealth formula is class-only, not "class + sliding doors + glass
  area".** Glass area is not a field and would itself be new research. Note the
  bullets genuinely conflict here: the Highlander (SUV) claims stealth, the
  RAV4 (SUV) denies it. The two-tier class formula (B4) takes a side: vans and
  the commuter hatchbacks are `high`, SUVs/wagons `medium`. If that's too
  coarse, the alternative is dropping the trait, not hand-rating.
- **F4 — enum-only compilation, with a note.** Decision 5's stated motivation is
  avoiding multi-valued tag arrays, and its YES template uses `op:'in'`.
  Threshold traits (B6, B7/B8, B9) could equally compile numeric `>=` rules on
  existing fields with zero new `FIELDS` entries — `evaluateGate` already
  supports it. I'm complying with the enum path: it keeps every picker row the
  same shape, the resulting criteria render properly in the existing enum
  multi-select editor, and the formula text lives in one place. Not an open
  question, just a recorded road not taken.

  **Revisited after implementation.** The editor reason above is retired: it was
  wrong twice over. Compiled trait criteria never enter `prefs.criteria`, so they
  never reach the criteria editor at all, and numeric rules render there anyway.
  What F4 actually rests on, and did not say, is that `sleeps_six_feet`,
  `tow_class` and `clearance_class` are **prose-parser vocabulary**, not just
  picker plumbing — the `SYSTEM` prompt maps categorical wants to them, and
  parser-emitted criteria on those fields *do* reach `prefs.criteria` and the
  editor. Removing them to save the enum machinery would leave any stored
  criterion naming them degrading to `unknown`: it would stop filtering with no
  error, in a shared blob. The enum path stands, on that reason.

  What the review did surface correctly: each threshold trait should have a
  numeric twin the parser can prefer when someone gives a specific number.
  `cargo_length_in` and `tow_max` already played that role; clearance was the only
  one without one, so `clearance_in` was added.
- **F5 — two vehicles read `unknown` on B6 by design.** The Santa Fe MX5 and
  Tucson NX4 cargo lengths were nulled as unverifiable (commit `d7f6b28`), so
  `sleeps_six_feet` is null there: they gate as unknown, never excluded, and
  show the existing "?" badge. That is the null-never-excludes rule working,
  not a bug.
- **F6 — the "AWD available" bullet doesn't map.** `drivetrain_bucket` reads
  the listed trim. The Pacifica's bullet about AWD *availability* on other
  trims is editorial (its listed trim is FWD): a per-record "AWD offered
  somewhere in the lineup" field would contradict the listed-trim convention
  every other field follows. The Sienna's AWD bullet maps fine — its listed
  trim is AWD.

## Decisions (Brandon, 2026-08-17)

- **Q1 — sit-up-inside: editorial, no trait.** The class formula is rejected:
  it would assert a physical fact the dataset can't support. Bullets stay as
  supporting text only.
- **Q2 — `onboard_ac_power` basis: "available on this generation from the
  factory, any trim or option."** Recorded in each record's `basis` and called
  out as a deliberate, documented divergence from the `equipment` block's
  standard-on-listed-trim rule (also noted in CLAUDE.md's schema section).
- **Q3 — DC fast charging is in; premium-fuel-required is dropped** (C6
  removed; the Metris bullet's premium-fuel half is editorial residue).
- **Q4 — thresholds accepted as proposed**: sleep 75/70 in, tow 2,000/3,500 lb,
  clearance 8.5/7.0 in, AC power 400/1000 W.
- **Q5 — embedded-data trim: pass-through, no `sync_vehicles.py` change**
  (`trim_vehicle` already copies unknown keys, matching the `equipment`
  precedent).

## Review additions (Brandon, 2026-08-17)

- **R1 — the evidence disclosure (plan Step 5) is required in v1, not
  severable.** 59 editorial bullets plus the residue prose are otherwise
  invisible in the picker; without them it reads as a spec filter, not the
  merged pros-and-cons list that was asked for.
- **R2 — the 13 Group A equipment rows live behind their own collapsed
  subsection**; Group B and C rows stay expanded by default.
- **A2/B2/B7 are YES-only rows** — a NO that means "rule out flat-floor-capable
  vehicles" is a control with no sane use, so `TRAITS` entries declare which
  sides are meaningful.
